# Spire rulings

The house rulings this session runs on. Players and referee both work from
this page, so there is nothing to argue about mid-climb. The underlying system
is on the [Tunnel Goons](help:tunnel-goons) page.

## Sky-blindness

One hour after you enter the tower you stop perceiving blue.

**The curse is the circle, not the tower.** The teeth are buried in a ring
around the crag, so it holds anywhere inside that ring and lifts once you walk
out of it. You can see the goblins' sky-blue tarp perfectly well on the way in,
and will not be able to find it from a window afterwards.

**Everything inside is sky-blind, not just you.** The undines, the giants, and
the goblin thieves are all in the ring. That is what makes a Ricalu cloak worth
taking: wearing one hides you from anything in the tower, undines included.

Killing the shaman does not lift it. The magic outlasts its caster by a day at
least.

This is not invisibility. Blue is **not reported**. Your brain fills the gap
with whatever is around it, so a blue tapestry over a doorway reads as blank
wall, and the wall looks perfectly ordinary. A thief in a blue cloak reads as
empty air with a floating head and hands. You do not experience a gap, and you
will not be told that something is missing.

- There is no roll to see through it. There is nothing to roll.
- A deliberate, physical, systematic search finds blue things **automatically**.
  Touching every foot of a wall finds the tapestry. Sweeping a floor with a
  spear finds the rug. No roll, but it costs time, and time is an encounter
  check.
- Attacking something you cannot see is **DS 12**.
- Say out loud when you are deliberately touching things. Hands still work.

The referee's ruling on the edge cases: the sky reads as a flat pale nothing,
the lake reads as grey, blue eyes read as brown, and a blue flame gives light
you can read by while you cannot see the flame. Water in the fountain is
visible as motion and wet, but something made of water holding still is gone.

There is an object in this tower that cancels sky-blindness for whoever wears
it. It is the most valuable thing in the building for anyone who understands
the problem.

## The sequence

Entering room 1 gives every character their own spark. From then on, entering
the room whose number is exactly one higher grows it a step, and entering any
other numbered room kills it, silently and immediately.

Never rolled. Track it yourself. Draw your own map and argue about it; that is
the game. The sparks are blue, so after the first hour nobody can see their
own, though you can feel the cold of it and other people's hair stands up near
a big one.

Understanding *why* it works is rollable: Erudite DS 10 in the study, DS 12
anywhere else.

## Chains

Every window from the second floor up has a rusty chain bolted to its sill,
dangling to the window below it on the same face. Window 4N drops to 3N.

| Climb | DS |
|-------|-----|
| Down one chain | 8 |
| Up one chain | 10 |
| Carrying a silver sack | 12 |
| Round to the next face | 12 |
| In wind or rain | +2 |

**No chain runs sideways.** They only go up and down, so getting from a window
on one face to a window on another at the same height means working along the
stonework, one face at a time, and a miss is a fall the whole way down. You
will need this: the roof chain hangs on the north face, and the way up to it
starts from a window that does not.

Rope, a hook, or climbing gear gives +1. A miss going up is a fall between
floors: 3 damage. A fall from a window to the ground is fatal.

Outside, height and direction mean what they say. The exterior never lies.

## The cone

Descend the forty feet of the changeling cone and you double in size for d6
days. Ascend and you halve. No roll, no warning, and nobody in the room notices
anything.

Doubled: +1 Brute, -1 Skulker, and you no longer fit through small openings.
Halved: +1 Skulker, -1 Brute, and larger things can swallow you without trying.

## Loot and encumbrance

A sack of silver is 4 items' worth of encumbrance and needs two hands or a
strap. Sparks are weightless, invisible, and the only thing worth carrying to
the top.

## Group actions

When the whole party climbs a chain or sneaks past something, everyone rolls.
Failures take the damage or make the noise.

## The altar

Bearing a full-grown spark to the rooftop altar completes the master spell. The
bearer takes **d6 + 4 damage, ignoring armour**, and then, if they live, gets
one wish. You are being told this in advance on purpose.

There is a limited number of wishes in the lake, and the referee rolls that
number in the open before the first one is spent.
