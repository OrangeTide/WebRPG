# Cairn spellbooks

216 spells, as a d666 table and as spellbook item cards. This is reference
material: it is always available in the module window, it is never installed,
and there is nothing secret in it.

## How Cairn treats magic

A spell is not something a character knows. It is an object. A spellbook holds
one spell, takes one inventory slot, and casting it means reading it. Anyone
who can read can cast, and losing the book loses the spell.

That travels well to systems where magic is an item rather than a class
feature, which is how [Tunnel Goons](help:tunnel-goons) works: magic is an
Erudite roll plus the right item. A spellbook is that item. Describe the
working, name the book, and take the consequence when you miss.

## Rolling for a spellbook

The table is d666: roll three d6 and read them in order, so 3, 1, 5 is entry
315. Every combination of digits 1 through 6 is a spell, which is why there are
exactly 216 of them.

Roll it when the party finds a spellbook and you have not decided what is in
it. The module window's Tables tab has a Roll button, then share the line you
rolled to chat.

## Dealing a spellbook

The Cards tab lists every spell as a spellbook card. Pick who it goes to and
deal it; it lands in their inventory as a one-slot item with the spell's effect
written on it. Use the filter box to find a spell by name or by what it does.

## Rulings worth making before play

Cairn's own rules for these are deliberately thin, and different tables land in
different places. Pick your answers up front:

- **Can two people read the same book?** In Cairn the book is the limit, so
  only its holder casts.
- **How often?** Cairn expects a cost, usually Fatigue, which fills an
  inventory slot. In a system without Fatigue, charge something else: a point of
  damage, a used-up book, or a complication the referee names.
- **Does the book survive?** Some spells here say what happens to the book when
  a save succeeds. Beyond those, decide whether books are permanent or burn out.
